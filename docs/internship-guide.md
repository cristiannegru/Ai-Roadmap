# 🎓 AI & ML Internship Guide

Securing a high-quality AI or Machine Learning internship requires a strategic combination of portfolio building, cold outreach, and technical interview preparation. This guide provides a step-by-step roadmap to land your first AI internship.

---

## 🗺️ Internship Timeline
```
[Months 1-3: Foundations] ──> [Months 4-5: Portfolio & OS] ──> [Month 6: Outreach & Applications]
            │                              │                                │
            ├─ Python & Math basics        ├─ Build 2 Core Apps             ├─ Custom LinkedIn outreach
            └─ Classical ML algorithms     └─ Contribute to open source     └─ System & Coding prep
```

## 📌 Step 1: Build Your Base Portfolio

Before applying to any role, you must have proof of work. Recruiters want to see that you can write clean, production-grade code.
* **Must-haves**:
  * Active GitHub profile with clean, well-documented code (no messy notebook files without explanatory READMEs).
  * At least two core AI applications deployed live (e.g., a RAG agent hosted on Render or Streamlit).
  * A personal portfolio page showcasing your projects, resume, and blog posts.

---

## 📌 Step 2: Where to Find AI Internships

1. **LinkedIn & Indeed**: Search keywords like *AI Engineer Intern*, *Machine Learning Intern*, *Generative AI Intern*, or *NLP Intern*.
2. **WellFound (formerly AngelList)**: The best platform for startup roles where you can communicate directly with founders.
3. **Y Combinator Work at a Startup**: Browse hundreds of high-growth, venture-backed startups looking for hungry interns.
4. **GitHub Repositories**: Many open-source companies list internship positions on their repository homepages or contribution guides.

---

## 📌 Step 3: Cold Outreach (The Secret Weapon)

Applying online to job portals has a $< 5\%$ success rate due to high applicant volumes. Cold outreach increases your odds by bypass-routing directly to hiring managers.

### ✉️ Cold LinkedIn/Email Template
Send this to Engineering Managers, AI Architects, or Startup Founders:

```text
Subject: AI Engineer Intern Opportunity - [Your Name]

Hi [Name],

I've been following [Company Name]'s work on [mention a specific project or feature they built, e.g., integrating local LLMs for customer support]. It's a fascinating approach.

I am an AI developer specializing in RAG architectures and model fine-tuning. I recently built [Name of project] which [mention what it does & include a live link]. 

I would love to contribute to [Company Name] as an AI Engineering intern this summer. I have experience with PyTorch, FastAPI, and Docker. 

Are you open to a brief 10-minute chat next Tuesday at 2 PM to see how I could add value to your team?

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Link to Portfolio / GitHub]
```

---

## 📌 Step 4: Cracking the Technical Assessment

The interview process for AI interns typically consists of three stages:
1. **Resume Screen & HR Chat**: Validating your interest and cultural fit.
2. **Coding Challenge / Take-Home Assignment**:
   * E.g., "Build a microservice that exposes an endpoint to analyze customer sentiments from a CSV."
   * *Tip*: Standardize code with logging, unit tests, and a clear `Dockerfile` to stand out.
3. **Technical Interview**: System design queries, machine learning fundamentals, and coding in Python.
